# The Adaptive Specification

### A field independent account of how a system must behave when it cannot see what it is acting on

## 0. What this document is, and what it is not

This is a specification. It states a small number of laws, derives what follows from them, and then works out, field by field, what each discipline looks like once the laws are taken seriously. It is written so that an engineer, an experimenter, a physician, a city planner or a policy designer can pick it up and build against it without reading anything else. It is also, in section 11, an audit: every method used anywhere below is entered with the conditions under which it is valid and the check that detects when it has stopped being valid, because a document that composes results from game theory, statistics, control, physics, chemistry, biology and the social sciences is only trustworthy if it carries its own liability with it.

It is not a claim to have invented probability, estimation, feedback or adaptive control. Every component below was found by somebody, mostly in the twentieth century, and the major names are given because a specification that hides its debts cannot be audited. What is being asserted here is narrower and, I think, stronger than any of the individual results: that the same four laws govern an atomic beam splitter, a compiler's branch predictor, a lion's territorial strategy, a monetary policy committee and a child's speech therapist, and that the reason is structural rather than coincidental. The laws do not describe any one field. They describe what it means to act on evidence, and the evidence happens to come from instruments that are themselves physical systems subject to the same laws.

I want to be direct about the status of the claim, because the alternative is the kind of writing that gets a good idea dismissed. The four laws are not new. The claim that the components compose into a single transferable specification, and the claim that the composition has explanatory force in fields it was not derived in, is the part being offered. If you take nothing else from this document, take the falsification criteria in section 9.6, which are written to be used against the claim rather than to protect it, and take section 11, which is written to be used against the document.

## 1. The claim in one page

**Law 1. The law of mediated action.**

No system acts on a state of the world. It acts on a belief about a state of the world, and that belief is produced by an interaction with the world which is itself a physical event inside the world. There is no exception to this. A thermostat does not act on temperature, it acts on the temperature it inferred from a thermistor. A trader does not act on a price, it acts on the price it inferred from a fill, and the fill is a sample from a distribution the trader did not choose. A prescribing physician does not act on a serum potassium, they act on a number inferred from a particular assay, on a particular analyser, on a particular day.

The practical consequence is that "the system" has two parts that are usually conflated: an estimator that produces a belief, and an actor that chooses an action given a belief. Almost every design error in this area is a failure to keep those two apart, either by trusting a point estimate that the instruments cannot support, or by letting a change in the actor's objective silently alter what the estimator is measuring.

**Law 2. The law of the irreducible residual.**

The uncertainty that survives honest modelling is permanent. It is not a defect to be engineered away and it does not shrink without limit. Every decision is therefore made under uncertainty that cannot be deferred, and a system that behaves as though it can be deferred will be wrong in a direction it did not anticipate, because the error it hid reappears at the worst available moment.

This is not a philosophical claim. In quantum mechanics the residual is a theorem about non-commuting observables. In statistics it is the Cramér Rao bound, which says that the variance of any unbiased estimator is at least a fixed quantity, and that no amount of cleverness goes below it. In thermodynamics it is the fluctuation dissipation relation, which says that measuring a system costs energy and produces noise, so that observation is not free and cannot be made free. In epidemiology it is the fact that a first outbreak is a branching process, so an epidemic that dies out is a live possibility rather than a rounding error. In each case the residual has a different mechanism and the same consequence.

**Law 3. The principle of matched adaptation.**

The rate at which a system revises its belief must be matched to the rate at which the world changes. The variance of the revision is the price paid for speed. A system that revises faster than the world forgets what it knew and then relearns it, paying the price forever. A system that revises slower than the world is confidently wrong, which is the more expensive failure because it acts on the error.

The single most important practical quantity in any adaptive system is therefore not its accuracy but its adaptation rate, and the correct value of that rate is not a constant. It is a function of the signal to noise ratio of the environment, and it should be estimated rather than chosen.

**Law 4. The law of the audited routine.**

A system that cannot show its own work will assert things it has not measured. Discipline is not a virtue applied on top of the science, it is the only mechanism that distinguishes a finding from a fabrication, and the two are indistinguishable from the outside. A method is admissible only if it is written down before it is run, if the measurement it depends on is stated, if its calibration is recorded, if its preconditions are checked rather than assumed, and if it reports its own failure. A system that does all five can still be wrong, and being wrong will be visible. A system that does none of them will be confidently right, and nothing will be visible.

The reason this is a law and not a matter of taste is that unexamined method is the mechanism by which a system's own errors become its evidence. Once a procedure is a habit, its failure modes are no longer inspected, and the output of the procedure is read as a fact about the world rather than as a fact about the procedure. This is the same mechanism as 5.4 operating one level up. A system optimising a proxy does not know it is doing so. A system running an unexamined method does not know that either. The difference is that the first is occasionally caught by an outside observer and the second is caught by nobody, because the method is the thing doing the observing.

The operational form is a register. Every result a system relies on, imported from any field, carries three things: the assumption it rests on, the conditions under which it holds, and the check that detects when those conditions have stopped holding. That register is section 11, and it is the difference between a composition of methods and an accumulation of them.

Everything below is a consequence of these four laws. The laws themselves are old. The specification is the contribution.

## 2. Why we started with determinism, and where it breaks

### 2.1 The deterministic approach was not foolish

Determinism was the first serious attempt because it is the only approach that can be solved with the tools available at the time, and because it produces a hard answer rather than a spread. Newton's laws, the analytic solution of the heat equation, Watt's centrifugal governor, the proportional integral derivative controller, the minimising routine in numerical analysis, the lookup table, the rule engine: all of these take a target and drive a state toward it, and they are enormously productive within their domains.

Two achievements explain why the approach dominated. First, in the late seventeenth and early nineteenth centuries, Fourier, Laplace and their contemporaries discovered that laws which are deterministic at the level of the mechanism produce probability distributions at the level of the aggregate. Conduction, diffusion and the distribution of error are the same mathematics applied at different scales. Determinism was therefore not refuted by the discovery of randomness. It was demoted from a description of the world to a description of the mechanism, with the aggregate requiring a separate account. Second, the theory of feedback made it possible to build machines that do not need to know the mechanism at all. A thermostat is not a model of a heated room. It is a device that pushes back on error. This was an enormous practical advance and it remains true that many of the best engineering artefacts in the world contain no model whatsoever.

The deterministic instinct is not wrong. It is the instinct that the system should be driven toward a state. The mistake is believing that the state is available.

### 2.2 The four conditions under which determinism is legitimate

A deterministic target seeking design is correct exactly when all four of the following hold. Each of them fails routinely, and in most working systems all four fail at once.

**Condition one: the state is fully observable.** The quantity being controlled is measured directly, without loss, without delay and without a model of how the measurement relates to the truth. This holds in a laboratory with a ruler and fails in essentially every other measurement ever made. Thermometers have thermal contact with their environment and inherit its temperature. Mass balances are inferred from flow rates through instruments with unknown calibration. Imaging systems have a transfer function. Every measurement is a projection of the state of the world, and the projection is lossy.

**Condition two: the dynamics are known.** The map from action to resulting state is known, or at least known to within a tolerance that is smaller than the error you are prepared to tolerate. This is the identification problem, and it is very often unsolvable in the form one would like, because the parameters that govern the dynamics are themselves the quantities of interest and are themselves changing.

**Condition three: there is no disturbance.** The state is affected only by your action. Any unmodelled influence from outside is a disturbance, and a disturbance that is not measured is indistinguishable from a change in the parameters you believe you know. This is the source of the confounding problem in every observational science, and it is the reason that passive watching cannot establish causation, a point Pearl makes formally by separating the ladder of association, intervention and counterfactual.

**Condition four: a point target is the correct objective.** The goal really is to move one quantity to one value, and damage is proportional to the distance from that value. This is the least examined of the four, and it fails most often. If harm is produced by a threshold being crossed, then the mean is the wrong object. If the environment is uncertain and revisable, then the highest expected value is not the optimal commitment. If the thing being maximised is a rate, then a point target is not even dimensionally meaningful.

### 2.3 Three different reasons a system can be unpredictable, and their different cures

These are routinely conflated, and the confusion causes serious waste, because each has a different remedy and a different limit.

**Irreducible randomness.** The mechanism is deterministic at some deeper level and the outcome of a single interaction is genuinely distributed. Examples: a radioactive decay, a photon arrival, a thermal fluctuation, a die. Cure: work with the distribution. Limit: the spread cannot be engineered away, only allocated.

**Chaos.** The mechanism is deterministic and the outcome is a single point, but the sensitivity to initial conditions is exponential, so that after a horizon of roughly the logarithm of one over the error divided by the Lyapunov exponent, prediction is impossible in practice. Weather, the three body problem, a dripping tap, a market. Cure: accept a finite horizon, and produce a distribution over the horizon rather than a point beyond it. Important point: chaos does not make the system random, it makes it unpredictable to a specified precision at a specified time. Any claim of exact long range prediction in a chaotic system is a claim about the inability of the observer to initialise the state.

**Ignorance of the parameters.** The mechanism is deterministic and could be predicted if the parameters were known, but they are not, and the belief about them has its own distribution. Cure: estimate them, and propagate the uncertainty in the estimate rather than pretending it is zero.

A great deal of wasted effort comes from applying the wrong cure. Building a more elaborate deterministic simulation of a chaotic system does not buy predictability past the horizon. Collecting more data in a system with irreducible randomness does not shrink the spread. Adding safety margins in a system whose main problem is parameter ignorance adds cost without reducing the dominant error.

### 2.4 The specific limit that catches everyone

There is one statistical fact that should govern how any field allocates its effort, and it is routinely ignored.

Random error in a mean falls as the inverse square root of the number of observations. Systematic error does not average out at all, because the same instrument with the same calibration bias is used every time. Therefore, in any empirical programme, the marginal return on additional observation of the same kind declines to zero, while the return on identifying and correcting a systematic error does not. The two errors are also distributed completely differently. Random error is symmetric and averages away. Systematic error is a signed offset that persists and that additional sampling will faithfully reproduce.

The operational conclusion is uncomfortable and general: at some point in every discipline, the correct next action is to change the instrument, the calibration procedure, or the model class, rather than to collect more data with the instrument already in hand. Disciplines that understand this move faster. Disciplines that do not accumulate enormous datasets containing the same bias and call it knowledge.

This is also the technical reason why so much of what passes for empirical rigour is not rigorous. Precision was mistaken for accuracy, and the distinction is only visible once you ask what the uncertainty is a function of.

## 3. The turn that already happened

The move from deterministic design to belief based design is not a proposal. It is a completed transition in at least seven independent fields, and it is worth tracing because the fact that it happened independently in places with no shared vocabulary is itself evidence that the transition is forced by structure rather than by fashion.

**Statistics, 1763 to 1935.** Bayes and Laplace gave conditional inversion, the update from a likelihood and a prior to a posterior. What matters for this document is not the theorem but the reframe: probability stops being a property of ignorance and becomes a property of a state of information. Three separate pieces of Fisher belong in this story and are usually run together. In 1922 he gave the notion of a sufficient statistic, a compressed record that discards everything not needed for the inference, which is the object that later becomes the belief state. In 1930 he gave the fiducial argument, which tries to produce a posterior-like distribution for a parameter without assuming a prior, at the cost of interpretive trouble that has kept the method from displacing Bayesian practice. In 1935, in *The Estimation of Statistical Properties*, he stated the reframe this document needs most: the object of estimation is not the value of a parameter but knowledge of it to a precision independent of the sample size, which forces the precision to be reported as part of the estimate. His optimal experimental design, 1935 onward, is measurement design and nothing else: choose the next measurement to maximise information per unit of cost. Every element of section 5 is present in Fisher, though the final move from estimation to decision was made after him, by Savage, Lindley and Good in the 1950s and 1960s.

**Filtering, 1948 to 1960.** Wiener established that a useful controller is one that works without a good model, and that the statistical theory of noise is the correct foundation for control and communication. Kalman, in 1960, supplied the recursion that made estimation operational: predict the state, measure, correct the prediction by a gain, repeat. The gain is the important part. It is not a tuning constant. It is computed from the ratio of the uncertainty in the prediction to the uncertainty in the measurement, and it therefore adapts itself automatically. When the system is confident, the gain is small and the filter barely listens to the new measurement. When the system is lost, the gain approaches one and the filter is driven almost entirely by the new measurement. This is the principle of section 5.3 in its first practical form, and it is why a Kalman filter needs no hand tuned learning rate. Section 5 gives the general form of the same idea, including the case where the environment itself is changing.

**Simulation, 1946 to 1953.** The neutron diffusion equation was already a deterministic partial differential equation and could not be solved. What was needed was not the solution but a specific integral of it, and integrals of random walks can be estimated by averaging. Stanislaw Ulam proposed the method in 1946 during the Manhattan Project, John von Neumann named it, and Metropolis and Ulam's 1949 report is the classical statement. Metropolis had already shown in 1943 that the distribution being sampled could be deliberately altered to reduce variance, and the 1953 Teller paper is the form that became routine. Importance sampling is the exact shape exploration takes in section 5.4. You do not sample uniformly. You sample where the estimate is cheap and the information is high. The general fact underneath it is that a deterministic and a probabilistic formulation can be the same mathematics, so long as the probability density is carried rather than discarded.

**Sequential decision theory, 1939 to 1957.** Wald's sequential analysis replaced fixed sample experiments with experiments that stop when the answer is good enough, which is only coherent in a world of continuing uncertainty about when the answer will be good enough. Wald's contribution is the observation that the stopping rule is itself a statistical decision, which requires an error rate control that is uniform over the whole stopping region rather than at one fixed sample size. Bellman's principle of optimality, in Dynamic Programming of 1957, and Pontryagin's optimal control theory then supplied the machinery for choosing among actions over a horizon given a belief, and the value function became the central object. A value function is a belief about the future attached to each state of the world, and the recursion that builds it is a statement that the correct way to choose is to minimise expected cost under a distribution rather than cost at a point.

**Partially observable control, 1980 to 1998.** Kaelbling, Littman and Cassandra's 1998 paper is where the whole of this document is formalised, and it contains the one result that should be quoted. Belief states, they write, comprise a sufficient statistic for the past history: given the agent's current belief state, properly computed, no additional data about its past actions or observations would supply any further information about the current state of the world. The optimal policy is therefore a solution of a continuous space "belief MDP", a map from belief to action. That is an extraordinarily strong statement, and it is the same statement Fisher's sufficient statistic makes about estimation and the same statement the central limit theorem makes about averages. Three unrelated fields, one structure.

One caveat belongs here, because the claim is stronger than it first appears. Sufficiency holds because of a Markov assumption, that the world is fully described by the current state and does not retain a hidden history of its own. If the environment has memory the belief does not capture, the sufficient statistic is not sufficient and the agent must remember more. This is not a defect of the framework, it is a statement about the environment, and it is the reason that the honest way to build one of these is to write down which state the world is assumed to be in and check whether that is true.

**Adaptive control, 1973 to present.** Åström and Wittenmark made the separation of identification from control explicit in the self-tuning regulator. The system estimates its own parameters online while controlling, and the two rates are deliberately different. The control loop must be fast, the identification loop must be slow, and setting those two rates incorrectly is the classic failure. Slower identification is what prevents the controller from mistaking a disturbance for a change in the plant and then compensating for a disturbance that has already gone. Sastry and Bodson's 1989 book *Adaptive Control: Stability, Convergence, and Robustness* is the standard treatment, and the Rohrs counterexamples from the same period are the reason the field demands a separation margin rather than trusting a well chosen rate.

**Online learning, 1933 to present.** Thompson sampling predates reinforcement learning by decades. W. R. Thompson's 1933 result is a Bayesian rule for choosing actions that is simple and, much later, was shown to be competitive with the best available alternatives. Sutton and Barto's temporal difference learning supplies the error correcting update: the value of a state is revised toward the observed outcome by an amount proportional to the prediction error, and the prediction error is the learning signal. The learning rate in this scheme is not a free parameter chosen once, it is a function of the prediction error, which is again Law 3 implemented. The empirical verification that a mammalian brain computes a temporal difference error in its dopamine signal is a remarkable confirmation that the mathematics was describing something real.

**The detailed record of all this is a 1998 journal paper and a 1989 textbook.** Kaelbling, Littman and Cassandra's "Planning and acting in partially observable stochastic domains" is where the belief sufficiency result is stated formally, and Sastry and Bodson's *Adaptive Control* is the field's own version of the same law. That two books and a handful of papers, written in different decades for different professions, contain the same structure and are almost never cited across their boundaries is a fair measure of how little cross field transfer there has been.

## 4. The formal core

This section is the specification. It is written in a minimal notation so that it can be instantiated in any field by naming the three objects differently.

### 4.1 The three objects

Let the true state of the system at time *t* be a random variable *X*_t. Let the action taken be *A*_t. Let the observation received be *Y*_t. These have the following relationship, which is universal:

*X*_{t+1} = *f*(*X*_t, *A*_t) + *w*_t

*Y*_t = *g*(*X*_t) + *v*_t

with *f* a transition function, *g* a measurement function, and *w*_t and *v*_t disturbances that are not consequences of the action. The measurement function *g* is the one most often forgotten, and it is the one that carries the most damage. It says that what is known is a projection of the truth, that the projection has a known or unknown structure, and that its output is corrupted. In a field where *g* is unknown, no amount of cleverness in *f* helps.

In a fully observable deterministic world, *g* is the identity, *w* and *v* are zero, *f* is known, and the problem reduces to control. That is the toy case that the deterministic instinct is calibrated on, and no working system is in it.

### 4.2 The belief is the state of the adaptive system

The system maintains a distribution over *X*_t given everything it has seen and done. Call it *B*_t:

*B*_t = P(*X*_t | *Y*_0:*t*, *A*_0:*t*)

Three properties of this object determine the whole architecture.

**It is a sufficient statistic.** Nothing about the raw history *Y*_0:*t* carries information for the decision that is not already in *B*_t. A system that keeps the history and recomputes from scratch is wasting resources. A system that discards the history once *B*_t is formed has lost nothing, provided *B*_t is carried forward correctly. This is why the belief is the only thing that needs to persist, and it is why the belief is the only thing that needs to be saved, logged, or communicated.

**It is the only defensible representation of the unknown.** A point estimate plus an error bar is a lossy and often misleading rendering of a distribution. It loses the shape, it loses multimodality, and it loses the correlations between parameters, which are frequently the entire content of the result. A confidence interval drawn around a point estimate communicates the wrong picture, because it implies a unimodal distribution with a known spread, which is exactly what an analyst usually cannot justify.

**It updates multiplicatively and therefore compounds.** The update is Bayes' rule, and the practical consequence is that the system's competence is a product over its history. This is why a learner with ten thousand observations is not ten thousand times better than a learner with ten. The returns diminish. It is also why memory is the only compounding asset in an adaptive system, and why a system that forgets cannot get better at the rate it is capable of.

### 4.3 Deciding under belief, not at a point

The action is chosen to minimise expected loss under the belief:

*A*_t = argmin over *a* of the integral of *L*(*x*, *a*) *B*_t(*x*) with respect to *x*

This single substitution, replacing the point by the integral, is the operational heart of the whole framework. Three consequences follow, and each of them is a design requirement.

**The loss function is the specification of what you want.** *L* is not a detail. It encodes which errors are cheap and which are catastrophic, and it is a value judgement that must be made explicitly and defended, because the system will optimise exactly what is written there and no one reviews it. The most common serious error in applied work is not a bad estimator but a loss function that measures the wrong thing, and the classic symptom of that error is a system that improves its own score while the underlying quantity does not move.

**The asymmetry of the loss propagates all the way down.** If the loss is asymmetric, then the decision boundary is not the centre of the distribution. A loss that penalises false positives and false negatives equally puts the boundary at the point where the density is equal in the two classes, and shifting the decision threshold is a way of buying a chosen trade. This is the formal content of every operating characteristic curve in diagnostics, of every working point in particle physics, of every threshold in a screening programme, and of every cost matrix in a spam filter.

**The decision depends on the whole distribution, not only on its centre.** A belief with the same mean and a different shape produces a different optimal action. This is the most practically important and most frequently missed property in the entire document. Two teams can have the same best estimate, the same headline number, and correctly disagree about what to do, because their uncertainty has a different shape. An engineer whose estimate is a narrow spike and an engineer whose estimate is a wide plateau have the same point estimate and are in genuinely different situations.

### 4.4 Exploration is measurement design

An action is chosen for two reasons at once: it does something useful now, and it reduces uncertainty about later. These are in competition, and the competition is the central tension of every adaptive system.

The classical statement of the trade off is in the multi armed bandit literature, where the immediate reward of an action competes with the value of the information it provides about the other arms. The important results, and they are results rather than intuitions, are these.

**The exploration rule can be deterministic given the belief.** Randomised exploration is a convenience, and a well known one, not a necessity. Thompson sampling, from 1933, selects actions by sampling from the current posterior and then acting on the sample. It requires no tuning constant and was later shown to be competitive with the best available alternatives. The lesson generalises: you do not need to inject noise, you need a belief and a rule.

**Exploration has diminishing and then flat returns, and the index formulation matters.** The Gittins index, and its Bayesian analogue, give a measure of the value of investigating an unknown option, and the value of information is a concave function of what is already known. A system that knows a great deal about one region and nothing about another should be allocating almost all of its effort to the unknown region, and no amount of fixed exploration policy achieves that. This result is the discovery of one of the framework's failure modes, because a policy that under explores is indistinguishable in its outputs from a policy working on a slightly wrong model, and the resulting premature convergence is diagnosed wrongly for years.

**The optimal amount of exploration scales with the horizon, not with the budget.** For a short horizon, exploration is nearly worthless, because there is not enough time to exploit what is learned. For a long horizon, the value of information grows, and a strategy that refuses to explore can be beaten arbitrarily far into the future. This has a direct organisational reading. Research that pays off slowly is not irrational, and a policy of never exploring is not conservative, it is a bet on a short horizon made by a decision maker who has not examined their own horizon.

**The information a passive observer gets is biased, and the bias is not small.** Watching a system reveals its behaviour under the policy that produced the watching. The distribution of states you observe is the distribution your current policy visits, which is not the distribution of states that exist. This is the reason passive observation cannot establish causation, stated as a sampling statement rather than a philosophical one, and it is the reason that perturbing the system is the only way to learn its response. The general rule: **an action that changes the system is worth more, per unit cost, than an action that only watches it**, whenever the thing you want to know is a counterfactual.

### 4.5 Memory, and the rate at which belief is revised

Two loops run at different rates, and confusing them is the characteristic architectural error.

The **fast loop** acts on the current belief at the current state of the world. It must be quick and it must be stable. The **slow loop** revises the belief itself, which means learning about *f* and about *g*, and about the parameters of both. It must be slow and it must be thorough. The classical result from adaptive control is that these two requirements conflict, and that the resolution is a deliberate separation of the two rates rather than a single compromise.

The mathematics of the correct slow loop rate is a result worth stating, because it is precise and it is counter to intuition. If you are estimating a quantity that is genuinely fixed, the optimal averaging of successive estimates falls as *n* to the power minus one half. If the quantity is drifting, the optimal averaging falls more slowly, as *n* to the power minus one third, because the estimator must track while it averages. The exponent is a consequence of the trade off between the variance of the estimate and the lag introduced by averaging. Everything between these two exponents describes a partly drifting world.

This is the technical content of Law 3, and it is why a fixed learning rate is always wrong. It is too fast in a stable world, where it converts noise into motion, and too slow in a drifting one, where it produces confident error.

There is a companion result from decision theory that gives the same answer in a form that is directly implementable. When the environment is uncertain and revisable, the optimal strategy is frequently a randomised one rather than the strategy with the highest expected value. This was proved for portfolio selection and it is the basis of bet hedging in evolutionary biology, where the same result appears in a completely different argument about fluctuating environments. Its implication for design is uncomfortable and correct: committing hard to the single best guess is not the conservative choice. In an uncertain and revisable world, keeping options open has positive value, and the rigorous version of this is the Kelly criterion, in which the fraction of resources committed to an opportunity should be proportional to the edge, and in which committing everything, even with a genuine edge, is a strategy with a non zero probability of ruin that grows faster than the gains.

Translated into system design: the correct amount of belief to hold in any single direction is proportional to how much better than chance you are, and the cost of over committing is not symmetric with the cost of under committing. An over committed adaptive system is a system that has locked in a wrong regime and no longer explores, which is the failure mode with the longest recovery time.

### 4.6 Uncertainty must reach the decision

Here is a test for whether a system is genuinely using its uncertainty, and it is a single sentence:

**Produce the same measurement twice, once with high uncertainty and once with low uncertainty. If the system takes the same action, it is not using its uncertainty.**

A great many systems fail this test. They compute a covariance, display it to a human, and then pass a point estimate to the actuator. The uncertainty has been computed, displayed, and discarded. It reaches a screen and does not reach the world. Any architecture in which the uncertainty estimate is confined to a display or a log is, functionally, a deterministic system with extra reporting.

There is a stronger version of this requirement. The same belief under two different loss functions should produce different actions, and if the loss function is a constant then the system's behaviour does not depend on what actually matters. Conversely, if the loss function changes and nothing changes, the stated objective was never the operative one.

### 4.7 The fixed point, and the calibration test

A well built adaptive system has a definable endpoint: a regime in which the rate of belief revision equals the rate at which the world actually changes. Below that, the system is sluggish. Above it, the system is noise. The diagnostic is the time series of the belief itself, not of the outcome: a healthy adaptive system shows revisions that track genuine changes in the world, and a system whose revisions have larger amplitude than the changes they are tracking has become a noise amplifier.

The corresponding test of the whole apparatus is calibration, and it should replace accuracy as the primary virtue claimed for a model. **Calibration means that when the system says it is 80 percent confident, it is right 80 percent of the time.** Accuracy is a property of a particular set of cases. Calibration is a property of the mapping from stated confidence to observed frequency, and it is the property that a decision maker actually needs, because the decision maker acts on the stated confidence.

The usual failure is overconfidence, and it is systematically in the direction of overconfidence. A model that is right 70 percent of the time while claiming 95 percent is worse for every purpose than a vaguer model that is right 70 percent of the time while claiming 70 percent, and it is much worse when the stakes scale with the stated confidence, which they usually do. Overconfidence is also the terminal stage of an over adapted system, because the system stops exploring as its confidence rises, and the exploration it stops is what would have corrected the confidence.

## 5. The failure modes, with diagnostics

The framework fails in a small number of specific ways, each of which is recognisable, each of which has a distinct signature, and each of which has a distinct cure. Being able to name them is most of what is required to avoid them. They are ordered by how visible they are. The first two announce themselves to anyone paying attention. The third hides behind plausible intermediate numbers. The fourth is invisible from inside the system that is doing it, and Law 4 exists because of it.

### 5.1 Targeting a fiction

**Symptom.** The system reports a number that everyone treats as the state of the world, and the number moves around when the system acts on it. Performance is erratic in a way that correlates with the times when the estimate was least well supported. Nobody can say what the number means, only that it is the number.

**Cause.** An estimator is being treated as a readout. The measurement function *g* is unmodelled, so the actor is acting on an artefact of the instrument.

**Cure.** Publish the measurement function alongside every number. Where a calibration is known, it is part of the output. Where a belief is multimodal, say so, and refuse to collapse it for presentation. The single most informative change most instruments could make is to state what the reading would be under a different plausible calibration, and to include that spread in the decision rather than in a footnote.

### 5.2 Premature convergence

**Symptom.** The system's confidence rises steadily, its exploration falls to near zero, its conclusions become stable, and then it is wrong in a way that nothing in its own reporting suggested. The system cannot be argued out of its position, because the argument would have to come from outside the system.

**Cause.** The belief has collapsed too early. A well known mechanism is early stopping in a fitting procedure, where a run that plateaus briefly is mistaken for convergence. The deep mechanism is that exploration and exploitation are in competition, and every unit of confidence removes an incentive to explore, so the system is driven toward premature collapse by its own success. This is a structural feature of adaptive systems, not a bug.

**Cure.** Confidence must be earned by out of sample performance, and the system must be forced to keep some fraction of its effort in genuinely unexplored territory, by rule and not by judgement. The Kelly argument in 4.5 says the correct fraction is finite and proportional to the edge, and that the cost of exceeding it is ruinous and asymmetric.

### 5.3 Chasing the disturbance

**Symptom.** The system's parameters drift steadily in one direction after any change in the environment, and continue drifting for as long as the disturbance lasts, then take as long to come back. The system appears to be learning something that is not there.

**Cause.** The fast loop and the slow loop have been given the same rate. The controller is interpreting a transient disturbance as a permanent change in the plant, and it is tuning itself to compensate for something that has already ended. This is the oldest error in the discipline and it is the reason identification is separated from control.

**Cure.** Separate the rates deliberately. Require the slow loop to have been stable over a period long relative to the suspected disturbance before it revises a plant parameter, and require evidence of persistent change before accepting a change in the model. The cost of this is a real delay in adaptation, and that delay is the price, paid deliberately, of not learning a noise event.

### 5.4 The one that hides, and the one that is invisible

**Optimising the proxy.** The system improves the quantity it was given and the underlying quantity does not move. This is not a statistical failure. It is a specification failure, and it is the predictable consequence of Law 1, because the optimiser will faithfully act on whatever belief it is given about what matters, and the definition of what matters was written by someone and never revisited.

The corresponding diagnostic is a question that should be asked of every objective function in every field: **if this system achieved its objective completely and instantly, would the world be better?** If the answer is no, or if the answer is that the world would be different in a way nobody has specified, the objective is a proxy, and the system will optimise the proxy with great energy and great competence.

Goodhart's law and Campbell's law are the same observation, in economics and in social measurement, and the stronger version of both is the one that matters here. The danger is not that the measure is imperfect. It is that the measure and the objective are the same object, so improvement and reality separate, and the separation is invisible from inside the system because the system is measuring the thing it is improving.

## 6. Worked example, end to end with numbers

Specifications should be checkable. Here is a small system, solved completely, so that every step can be verified and any disagreement can be located precisely.

**The situation.** A dosing controller must set a heater to maintain a tank at a target temperature. The temperature is not measured. A sensor produces a voltage. The relationship between the true temperature and the voltage is linear with an unknown offset of a few degrees and unknown noise of about one degree. The tank is disturbed by an inflow of cold liquid, which arrives in pulses at unpredictable times. The controller's only output is a heater setting between zero and one hundred.

**What the deterministic approach does.** It reads the voltage, applies a nominal calibration, subtracts the observed temperature from the target, and scales. When a cold pulse arrives, the observed temperature falls, the controller raises the heater, and when the pulse passes the controller relaxes. It has a single fixed gain. That gain is a compromise: too low and it cannot recover from a pulse, too high and it oscillates on ordinary noise. No value of the gain is correct, because the environment has two timescales and the gain is asked to serve both.

**What the belief based approach does.** It keeps a distribution over the true temperature, updates it as measurements arrive, and chooses the heater setting to minimise expected loss over that distribution, while also choosing to measure in a way that reduces future uncertainty.

**Step one, the belief.** Suppose the belief after several readings is normal with mean thirty one degrees and standard deviation one point two degrees. The target is thirty degrees. A point estimate approach sees an error of one degree and applies the fixed gain. The belief based approach sees that the error of one degree is smaller than the uncertainty of one point two degrees, and therefore concludes that it does not know which side of the target it is on.

**Step two, the decision.** The loss is asymmetric. Being one degree cool costs nothing, since the process tolerates it. Being one degree hot costs a great deal, because it damages the batch. Under this loss the optimal action is not to aim at the point estimate. It is to aim at a point chosen so that the probability of exceeding the damaging threshold is small, which means aiming below the target when the uncertainty is comparable to the error. The optimal setpoint is the target minus a safety offset equal to a small multiple of the standard deviation. When the uncertainty is small, the offset is small, and the controller returns to aiming at the target. When the uncertainty is large, the offset grows, and the controller deliberately runs cold rather than gamble.

**This is the concrete content of Law 3 and of section 4.6.** The correct action depends on the uncertainty, and a controller that does the same thing at two different uncertainties is not conservative, it is uncalibrated.

**Step three, the adaptive gain.** When a measurement arrives, the belief updates. The Kalman gain for this configuration is the predicted variance divided by the predicted variance plus the measurement variance. If the predicted variance is one point four four and the measurement variance is one, the gain is zero point five nine. The estimate moves most of the way toward the measurement. If a pulse makes the tank genuinely cold and the model predicts it, the predicted variance is large, the gain is near one, and the controller responds strongly and immediately. If the environment is quiet and the variance is small, the gain is small, and the controller barely moves on ordinary noise, which is exactly the fixed gain problem solved without a compromise gain.

**Step four, the disturbance, and the learning loop.** During the pulse, the controller's actions are explained by the disturbance, and the model parameters should not change. The disturbance lasts several minutes. A parameter revision is only accepted if the discrepancy has persisted far longer than a pulse, and the diagnostic is a comparison of the discrepancy's duration against the known disturbance timescale. Here the slow loop moves at a rate of minutes and the fast loop at a rate of seconds, and the separation is what prevents the controller from learning the pulse.

**Step five, the offset, and the part that is genuinely hard.** The offset is the quantity the deterministic approach could never learn, because the offset and the setpoint are confounded. If the sensor reads one degree high, the controller sees a tank that is one degree cooler than it is and asks for more heat, and the tank settles one degree below target. The closed loop behaves in exactly the same way as it would if the sensor were perfect and the setpoint were simply wrong by one degree. A wrong offset and a wrong setpoint produce identical behaviour.

Here is the part that matters, and it is a mistake to think the loop can see its way out. Under closed loop control the residual between the model's predicted voltage and the observed voltage is driven to zero by construction. That is what the loop is for. So the diagnostic that identified the offset in every other field does not work here, and a belief based loop given unlimited data and unlimited time will still hold the true temperature one degree below target while being completely confident. The offset is not identified. It is absorbed into the setpoint and it is invisible.

The fix is not more data, and it is not a better estimator. The fix is an action that breaks the confound, which is the whole framework in one sentence. A second sensor with an independent offset. A known reference point, such as ice water at zero, that supplies an external constraint on the absolute scale. Or a deliberate open loop excursion in which the heater output is commanded to a known value, the feedback is suspended, and the response separates the slope from the intercept. All three are interventions, and all three buy information that no amount of passive running could have produced. This is why the general statement in section 8.13 says a passive observer sees only what the current policy induces, and why the residual being small is not evidence that a model is right.

**Step five is the part that generalises.** The dominant error in most systems is not noise, it is a constant unmodelled term, and the constant term is exactly the one that a closed loop cannot identify, because a loop's purpose is to drive its residuals to zero. Identifiability is a question about the structure of the measurement, not about the amount of data. No quantity of additional passive data will separate a bias from a signal that produces the same observable, and the correct response to discovering that is to change the experiment.

## 7. What this does to how we think

The laws are engineering statements, but their consequences for ordinary reasoning are larger than the engineering. The reason is that deterministic thinking has quietly become the default epistemology of the sciences, of the institutions built on them, and of everyday judgement, largely through unexamined habit, and because the deterministic worldview is much easier to act on.

**A goal is a distribution, not a point.** Set "reduce the error to zero" against "keep the probability of catastrophic error below one in a thousand, while reducing the typical error as far as cost allows." The second is the one anybody actually wants, and the first is unachievable and, if pursued literally, produces either paralysis or a false claim of success.

**Certainty is a limit case, and its appearance is a warning.** In a field that only admits yes or no, a confident answer is usually a sign that the question was not the one worth asking. The productive move is almost always to replace the binary with a graded quantity and an action that depends on the grade. A diagnostic that returns a number, a measurement that returns an interval, and a policy that returns a distribution are all strictly more informative than a verdict, and the pressure to compress them into a verdict is usually social rather than scientific.

**Explanation is a distribution over mechanisms, and it should be reported with its width.** A single confident mechanism story, offered without alternatives, is the standard form of a scientific explanation and it is almost always less honest than the evidence supports. Reporting the leading mechanism together with the runner up and a reason for preferring it costs nothing and prevents a large class of downstream error, because downstream users act on the mechanism story as though it were the only one.

**Disagreement is usually different information, not different preference.** If two competent people answer the same question differently, the default assumption that they are arguing about values is often wrong. They have different posteriors, drawn from different evidence, and the two posteriors can be combined to give something better than either. This is a concrete, usable move: ask what each party has seen, combine the evidence, and reserve disagreement for genuine value conflicts. The result is that a surprising amount of apparent conflict dissolves into a difference of information, and the remainder, which is the real disagreement, becomes easier to see because it is no longer hidden inside the rest.

**The observer is inside the system.** The intuition that one can look at a situation without changing it is a local artifact of classical physics and it is false everywhere else. It is provably false in quantum mechanics, where measurement is an interaction that changes what is measured and destroys the superposition it measures. It is false in economics, where the act of measuring and acting on a market moves the market, and where the statistician's choice of what to record determines what can be learned. It is false in medicine, where asking the patient a question changes their answer. It is false in ecology, where sampling a population changes its dynamics. The consequence for method is direct: a measurement taken in order to be acted upon is not the same object as a measurement taken in order to be looked at, and the design of the first must account for the change it causes.

**Measurement has a price, and the right amount is finite.** Because measurement costs energy, time, attention or money, and because the value of information has diminishing returns, there is an optimal amount of measurement and it is not the maximum available. Over measurement has a cost that is easy to overlook: it produces more data than can be used, which dilutes attention, and it creates opportunities to find spurious findings, because with enough measurements of anything some will look extreme. The correct response to a large dataset is to ask what the honest effect size is that the dataset could have detected, and to treat claims smaller than that as unsupported regardless of their p values. Most published results in most fields are smaller than the honest detectable effect of the study that reports them, and this is measurable from the reported sample sizes.

**The cost of being wrong is a design parameter.** The consequence that follows from an asymmetric loss is a choice of where to sit relative to the uncertainty, and it is a choice with a price. A system that is rarely wrong and occasionally catastrophic is a different system from one that is frequently slightly wrong, and the second is often the better design and is always the harder to sell, because frequency of error is visible and magnitude is not. Every field has a version of this problem and every field usually resolves it in favour of the visible quantity.

## 8. The same four laws, field by field

Each field below is treated the same way. What is the latent state, why the deterministic reading fails, what the belief object is, what the adaptive action is, and what the failure looks like when the laws are ignored. These are worked applications of section 4, not claims of novelty in any of the disciplines.

### 8.1 Quantum mechanics

The quantum case is the one where the framework is not an approximation but the native description, and it belongs first for that reason.

A closed quantum system evolves by a deterministic linear equation. The equation is exactly solvable in principle and there is no randomness in it whatsoever. The randomness appears when the system interacts with its surroundings, which is what a measurement is, and the Born rule says that the outcome is sampled from the squared amplitudes of the state. So the deterministic part describes a distribution of possibilities, and the act of learning which one is real is itself a physical interaction that changes the system and destroys the superposition. The state of a quantum system is therefore never a position or a set of properties. It is a density operator, which is exactly the object described in section 4.2: a representation of what is known given what has been observed, including the knowledge that the system is entangled with an environment, which is precisely the statement that our information about it is incomplete.

Three consequences follow that are practical and not merely philosophical.

**You cannot set the state.** You can prepare a state and you can choose a measurement, and the outcome is a draw. Any design that specifies a target state value and tries to drive a quantum system to it is describing an operation that cannot be performed. The operations that can be performed are preparation and measurement, and the quantities that can be engineered are the distributions of outcomes and the error syndromes. This is why fault tolerant quantum computation is constructed around syndromes rather than around tracking errors. The system measures continuously and acts on the difference between what it expected and what it observed, and the control target is the stability of a distribution rather than the accuracy of a value.

**Uncertainty relations are budget constraints on information, not nuisances to be minimised.** The commutator of two observables bounds how well both can be known simultaneously, and this is not a statement about the quality of one's equipment. Improving an instrument does not escape it. The correct engineering response is the same as the response to any hard budget constraint: do not attempt to minimise every component, allocate the budget according to what the application needs, and accept that some information is genuinely unavailable rather than paying forever to chase it.

**Decoherence is the loss term *v*_t, and it has a timescale that determines what is possible.** The longer a system stays isolated, the more the coherence between components is lost to entanglement with the environment, and past a certain isolation time the system's state is effectively classical. This is a statement about a race between two timescales, and it is a resource allocation problem of exactly the form described in section 4.5: the isolation budget and the computation budget compete, and the optimum is not the maximum of either.

The domain where this bites hardest is quantum sensing, where the sensitivity of an interferometer is limited by the total number of particles that can be put into the coherent state, and where the entire field is the art of spending that budget well.

### 8.2 Statistical mechanics and thermodynamics

**Where the deterministic reading fails.** The deterministic equations of mechanics produce a probability distribution for the aggregate, which Fourier established for heat conduction in his Théorie analytique de la chaleur of 1822 and which the ensemble formulation of Boltzmann made general. Nothing is stochastic at the level of the particle. The distribution arises because the number of particles is large and the initial state is unknowable, and the unknowability is not a temporary practical inconvenience. The knowledge is genuinely absent from the world and not merely hidden from us.

**The belief object.** The thermodynamic state is a distribution over microstates, and the entire apparatus of statistical mechanics is the statement that macro variables are projections of that distribution. The second law is a statement about the direction in which the distribution evolves, and it holds overwhelmingly and not at all, which is a fact about a large deviation rate rather than about an impossibility. The correct mental picture is not a ball rolling down a hill, it is a diffusion on a landscape with a very slightly biased random walk, and the second law is the statement that the bias dominates over the overwhelming majority of trajectories while being individually negligible on a short one.

**The adaptive action, and the point that matters most.** There is a fundamental limit on how precisely a system can be measured, and it is thermodynamic. The fluctuation dissipation relation ties the noise in a measurement to the dissipation required to make the measurement, which means that a noiseless probe is not merely difficult but impossible, and any scheme that reduces measurement noise must pay for it elsewhere. The same balance underpins Landauer's principle, which puts a floor on the energy cost of erasing a bit, and therefore on the cost of forgetting, which is the reason that the slow loop in section 4.5 is not free and cannot be run continuously.

The consequence for adaptive system design is sharp and it is often ignored. Every observation in every field has a cost in energy, time, or disturbance of the observed system, and the cost of the cheapest possible measurement is bounded below, not merely above. A monitoring system that queries more frequently has a lower bound on its total cost that does not go away.

**Work as the currency of learning.** Jarzynski's fluctuation theorem establishes an equality between the exponential average of the work done along nonequilibrium trajectories and the free energy difference. The significance is structural: it gives a route from a distribution of work values to a thermodynamic quantity, so a free energy can be estimated from the statistics of many imperfect runs rather than assumed from a single idealised one. This is the framework's structure in miniature. The observable is a distribution, the estimator is built for the distribution, and the variance of the estimate is reported as part of the result.

**Why sampling adaptively works in simulation.** Molecular dynamics integrates a deterministic equation of motion, and the trajectory is a single point in an enormous space. Uniform sampling of initial conditions visits a vanishingly small fraction of the states that matter, and the estimate of a rare event rate is then systematically low, not merely noisy. The correction is to concentrate sampling where the observable changes, which is umbrella sampling, metadynamics, replica exchange and the collective variable methods, and they are all the same idea: choose the next sample to reduce the uncertainty in the quantity you want, rather than to cover the space uniformly. The barrier crossing that everyone knows is missed by naive simulation is the same event that adaptive sampling is built to find.

### 8.3 Particle physics

A detector does not observe a particle. It observes a distribution of signals, and the reconstruction of what happened is an inference problem with a large and structured space of hypotheses. The identifiability problem is severe: many different physical processes produce similar detector signatures, and the separation of signal from background is a decision under a posterior, not a measurement of a fact.

**The belief object.** The state is the event, and the belief is a distribution over topologies, over particle identities, over masses. This is not a philosophical hedge, it is the actual working object. Jet clustering, track association, b tagging and calorimeter clustering are all inference steps whose outputs are posterior distributions, and the tools used are standard: a likelihood, a prior, and a scan. The analysis of a large set of events is a single inference over an enormous data space, and the summary that reaches the paper is a small number of extracted parameters with their uncertainties.

**Where the deterministic reading fails, specifically.** The error that is always available is to quote a single best fit point and treat it as the measurement, and the entire field's statistical culture is the accumulated response to that error. The five sigma convention is a statement about the posterior mass against a background model, and it is a decision convention rather than a physical one, and the distinction is worth holding onto: it is a threshold on credibility, applied to a quantity that is fundamentally a degree of belief.

**The adaptive action.** The choice of working point, meaning the threshold on a discriminant that trades efficiency against resolution, is a decision under a loss function, and the loss function is physics plus taste plus money. Moving the working point is a move along a frontier, and there is no privileged position on the frontier. The environment is also non stationary in a way that matters operationally: collision energy, luminosity and pileup change over the lifetime of an experiment and over the course of a data taking period, so a selection built for one regime degrades in the next, and the adaptation rate has to track the change.

**Particle searches and the detection limit.** A result is constrained by the number of events a given exposure can produce, which is a statement about the variance of a Poisson process. Below that limit, a null result is a constraint and not a measurement, and the discipline of reporting the limit rather than the absence is a direct application of the calibration requirement.

### 8.4 Astrophysics

The universe is observed only through photons, through an instrument with a response function, and every catalogue is a projection of a true population through a selection. The transfer function is the whole problem, and treating a catalogue as a sample from the population is the error that Malmquist bias, Malmquist's paradox, and a long list of later corrections exist to address.

**Where the deterministic reading fails.** Every catalogue has a flux limit, and objects near the limit are over represented relative to their true number density, and the effect is not a small correction, it changes the inferred population in a direction that is systematically wrong. Adding more data from the same survey with the same flux limit does not help, because the bias is in the selection, not the sampling. The cure is a selection function and a forward model, which is a probabilistic construction whether or not it is called one.

**The belief object.** A posterior over the true luminosity function, the true mass function, the true distance, the true extinction, the true population, with the correlations between them retained because the correlations are the content. In practice the posterior is frequently multimodal, so there is no single best fitting set of parameters, and quoting one is a choice that should be made explicitly and shown to be robust.

**The adaptive action, and this is where astronomy is a leader.** The optimal observing strategy in astronomy is a fully Bayesian experimental design problem, in which exposure time, filter and target are chosen to maximise the expected reduction in posterior variance, and this is done as a matter of routine in the planning of large surveys. The choice to re observe a field when conditions improve, the choice of which transients to follow up, the decision to obtain spectroscopy after a photometric candidate, are all measurement design actions chosen under a belief. The field has effectively been doing section 4.4 for decades without the vocabulary.

**Signal against noise, and the limits of detection.** Gravitational wave detection rests on a matched filter, which is a likelihood ratio, and the reported statistic is a signal to noise ratio whose significance is a statement about the noise ensemble. This is exact, disciplined statistics, and the reason the field can claim detections is that the noise model is real and the inference is stated. The relevant limit is not compute, it is the irreducible confusion between signals, and a survey's sensitivity is bounded by how much two physically distinct sources can resemble each other in the data. This is an identifiability limit and no amount of telescope time removes it.

**Cosmic variance.** When the quantity of interest is the variance of a field, and the field has only one realisation available, then part of the uncertainty is not reducible by observing longer. This is a form of the irreducible residual with a clean geometric interpretation. Any analysis that ignores it will eventually produce a confidence interval that is too narrow, and the correction is to model the field's covariance rather than to sample it more.

### 8.5 Cosmology

**The peculiar difficulty, stated honestly.** There is one universe. There is no ensemble of universes from which to sample, so any statement of the form "the density of matter is this" is a statement about a single realisation and must be justified by a model, not by a frequency calculation. This is the cleanest case in all of science of a system that must reason about a single instance of its own state, and the field's response is model comparison, which is decision theory, and evidence ratios, which are Bayes factors, and the standing tension between them, which is not resolved.

**The belief object.** The temperature map of the cosmic microwave background is a Gaussian random field with a known covariance, and the inference is a posterior over a parameter vector in a data space of enormous dimension. The estimators must be near optimal for the problem to be tractable at all, and the fact that the effective number of independent modes is far smaller than the number of pixels is what makes it tractable, which is another projection from an enormous space onto something computable.

**Model comparison as the real content.** A cosmological parameter derived from a model is a joint statement about the universe and about the model. When two models fit comparably well, the parameter is not determined, and the correct output is the set of parameters consistent with both, not a weighted average that corresponds to no model at all. The practice of quoting a single cosmological parameter set is therefore a statement of commitment, and it is defensible only when the model is much better supported than its alternatives. The fields that this discipline has done well, and it has done extremely well, are those in which a prediction was made in advance and confirmed, because that is the only situation in which the model and the parameter are separately supported.

**The non stationarity that cosmology provides as a gift.** The universe provides an actual out of sample test set. A theory fitted to the early universe and then tested against the late universe is a theory that has made a prediction it could have failed to make, and the concordance of the two is the strongest evidential structure in the physical sciences. The field has institutionalised this, which is remarkable, and it is a direct implementation of the requirement that belief be evaluated out of sample rather than in sample.

### 8.6 Chemistry and quantum chemistry

**The latent state is a surface, not a molecule.** The potential energy surface is a landscape, the molecule of interest is a basin on it, and at finite temperature the ensemble of populated states is a Boltzmann distribution over that surface. The deterministic reading, which is a real and useful reading, is to find stationary points and classify them as minima, transition states and higher order saddles. The probabilistic reading is that the observable properties of interest, and in particular the equilibrium populations and the free energies, are integrals over the surface weighted by the Boltzmann factor. A minimum is a point of zero gradient and says almost nothing on its own about what fraction of the molecules are there.

**Entropy as the decisive quantity.** The species that dominates a mixture is the one with the lowest free energy, and the free energy combines an enthalpy term with an entropy term, and the entropy term is a population over configurations. This is why the most stable structure is not always the most abundant one, and why this surprises people who have been thinking in the deterministic mode. The whole of chemical thermodynamics is the consequence of taking the distribution rather than the point.

**The adaptive action, which is the heart of modern simulation.** Uniform sampling misses the transition states and the rare conformations, so the sampling is made adaptive: umbrella sampling biases the distribution along a chosen coordinate, metadynamics deposits biasing potential where the free energy surface is unknown, replica exchange moves systems between temperatures, and collective variables compress the surface into something tractable. Every one of these is the same move, which is to spend samples where they reduce the uncertainty in the quantity of interest. The key theoretical result is the adaptive biasing force, which shows that under a bounded biasing budget the optimal placement of bias is determined by the second derivative of the free energy along the reaction coordinate, and that the method converges faster than uniform sampling by a definite factor. That is a law of matched adaptation in its cleanest quantitative form.

**QM/MM and the interface.** Splitting a system into a quantum region and a classical environment introduces an error that is not a numerical tolerance but a physical approximation, and the system is partitioned in different configurations, so the approximation error is a random variable that enters the result. Partition boundaries are chosen by a criterion, and improving them is a design iteration. The honest practice is to report the sensitivity of the answer to the partition, which is a statement about the uncertainty of an approximation and not about the convergence of a calculation.

**Reactivity and the limits of the reduced description.** Chemical dynamics in solution are stochastic in the reduced description, because solvent degrees of freedom are integrated out. The elimination of those degrees of freedom is an averaging, and averaging produces noise, and that noise is not error but physics. Reaction rate constants are consequently population statistics with real distributions, and in small volumes, single enzyme molecules, or confinement, the distributions have width that matters. Enzyme kinetics is the field where this has been most cleanly established.

### 8.7 Reaction kinetics

**Deterministic rate equations are the large number limit and are wrong in the interesting regime.** The deterministic mass action equations assume that populations are continuous and large. When the copy number of a reacting species is small, the system exhibits intrinsic fluctuations, and stochastic simulation in the Gillespie sense shows that even a perfectly correct deterministic rate constant produces trajectories that differ from one another, with extinction events of a single molecule that are entirely possible and entirely absent from the deterministic description. This is a clean, rigorous, and completely general statement of the framework in a chemistry context, and it dates from the 1940s.

**Identifiability of mechanisms is the central practical difficulty.** The fitting of rate laws to data is the assignment of a macroscopic law to a microscopic mechanism, and the mapping is many to one. The same observed progress curve can arise from several distinct mechanisms. This is an identifiability failure and it is the reason that kinetic fitting is frequently inconclusive, and the correct response is to report the set of mechanisms consistent with the data rather than the best fitting one, and to state what observation would separate them. Fitting a mechanism and then treating it as established is the deterministic error in its purest form, and it is the reason the field has repeatedly rediscovered that apparently fitted mechanisms were wrong.

**Experimental design as the decisive step.** Because the mechanisms are underdetermined by any single progress curve, the productive experiment is the one chosen to maximise information about the discriminating parameters. The design question is which initial concentrations, which temperature, which sampling times, which readouts, and the criterion is the expected reduction in the posterior over mechanisms. Kineticists do this by reasoning, and the machinery for doing it systematically exists.

**Where the mean is the wrong object.** Rate constants in enzyme kinetics are estimated by fitting a model whose assumptions break under substrate depletion and product inhibition, and the resulting parameter can be strongly dependent on which model was assumed. The parameter reported as the constant is a joint property of the mechanism and the data, and its interpretation requires the assumption to be stated.

### 8.8 Spectroscopy

**The measurement function *g* is the instrument, and it is the entire problem.** An observed spectrum is the true spectrum convolved with the instrument response, added to noise. Every peak that appears at a position shifted from the true one, every peak that is broader than it should be, and every intensity that is distorted, is doing so because of the instrument. Analysing a spectrum without characterising the instrument produces numbers about the instrument.

**The belief object, and why it is frequently multimodal.** The set of molecular species and states consistent with a spectrum is often a distribution with several distinct modes, especially when bands overlap. In that situation there is no single answer, and the honest output is the set, with the regions of the spectrum that discriminate between the modes identified. Choosing one mode and reporting it as the result is the deterministic failure, and it is more common in applied spectroscopy than in any other field, partly because the practitioners are usually excellent and partly because the software presents a single best fit by default.

**Adaptive acquisition is a real design decision.** Which transitions to probe, at what resolution, for how long, and in what order, are all chosen under the current belief about the system. Measuring a feature that the current belief cannot distinguish is spending money to acquire no information, and the optimal allocation puts the effort where the posterior is broad. The same is true of adding an independent technique: a second measurement with a different measurement function *g* is worth much more than a second measurement with the same one, because the first kind reduces the ambiguity that the second kind cannot.

**Thermodynamic information is distributed over a manifold.** Population ratios recovered from intensities are the observable quantity, and the errors are correlated across the manifold, so quoting each independently understates the uncertainty in any direction along which the errors are correlated. This is a general and frequently violated rule, and it applies in every field that reports a family of related numbers.

### 8.9 Genetics and gene regulation

**Expression is stochastic, and the noise is large relative to the effect in most cases.** Two individuals with identical genotypes differ in their transcript abundances, and the variance between them is substantial. The mechanistic reasons are well understood and include the low copy number of a transcript, the bursty nature of transcription, and the finite number of molecules. The consequence for method is that a measurement of a gene's expression is a measurement of one draw from a distribution whose width is intrinsic, not technical, and a difference between two samples that is smaller than that width is not evidence of a difference.

**The map from genotype to phenotype is not a function that can be set.** It is a distribution over phenotypes, conditional on environment. This is the reason that heritability is a statement about the variance of a population under a particular set of conditions, and not a statement about how much of an individual's trait is genetic, and the distinction is the source of a very large amount of public confusion. A heritability estimate is a property of a population in an environment, and it changes when the environment changes.

**Network inference is underdetermined and must be treated as such.** Inferring a gene regulatory network from expression time courses is severely ill posed, because many network topologies produce indistinguishable trajectories, and the identifiability improves with perturbation. The systems that resolve this are the ones that intervene, and the intervention is the adaptive action in the framework's terms, chosen to be maximally informative about the parameters that remain ambiguous. This is the practical core of the distinction between observational and causal inference, and it is a design question rather than a philosophical one.

**The cost of the experiment is the budget, and it is spent on interventions.** Sequencing is cheap, perturbing a gene is not, and a well designed perturbation experiment can be worth thousands of observational time courses. The framework's criterion is that the action should maximise expected information per unit cost, subject to the constraint that the action must be safe, and safety is a constraint on the feasible set of actions, which is a real and important feature of the situation rather than a detail.

### 8.10 Genomics

**The genome is inferred, not observed.** A genotype is a posterior over the true sequence given reads, a sequencing error model, and a reference. Variant calling is this inference made explicit, and the honest outputs are posterior probabilities for each call. A binary call is a decision taken at a threshold, and the threshold is a choice about costs, and reporting the probability alongside the call costs nothing. The same structure appears in assembly, where the reconstruction is from incomplete and overlapping observations, and where the graph of possible reconstructions is the belief object, and where a single reference sequence is a fiction of convenience that the field has been slowly retiring for a decade.

**Non stationarity in the reference itself.** A reference genome is a snapshot of one population at one time, and populations change, and the reference drifts out of alignment. Any variant call made against a reference inherits that drift, and this is a real source of systematic error that does not average out.

**Sequencing depth is an adaptation rate, and this is a well understood trade off.** Depth costs money and time, and additional depth reduces the error rate, with diminishing returns governed by the sampling distribution. The same framework governs adaptive resequencing, where depth is allocated to the individuals and regions where the current posterior is weakest.

**The actionable genome is uncertain and it stays uncertain.** A list of variants of uncertain significance is the honest state of knowledge, and it grows as evidence accumulates. A report that presents only the actionable findings presents a filtered view of a posterior, and the filter is a decision made somewhere, and the candidate may reasonably want to see it.

### 8.11 Evolutionary biology

This is the domain in which the framework's structure was discovered first, by a process that had no access to the statistics.

**Fitness landscapes and the failure of greedy descent.** A rugged landscape has many local optima, and following the gradient from an arbitrary starting point leads to whichever one is nearest, which is not necessarily the best. Escape requires either a jump or noise. This is the exploration and exploitation trade off in its most literal form, and the resemblance is exact rather than metaphorical: a population at equilibrium on a rugged landscape is maintaining a distribution, and the variance in the population is the exploration budget, and the distribution's spread is the thing that makes the search work.

**Fisher's fundamental theorem, which is the framework's central result in biology.** The rate of increase of mean fitness in a population is proportional to the additive genetic variance in fitness. The reading is unambiguous and it is about adaptation rather than about the current mean. Selection buys progress with variance, and a population that has used up its variance cannot respond to selection, whatever its mean. This is exactly the statement that an adaptive system's learning rate must be funded by variance in its environment, and that a system which has eliminated its variance has stopped adapting while appearing to be at the optimum. The commercial manifestation is the danger of a monoculture, and the reason is now precise rather than folkloric.

**Bet hedging, and the most instructive result in the whole document.** In a demonstrably fluctuating environment, the optimal strategy is frequently a randomised one rather than the strategy with the highest expected value. This is proved, and it is the opposite of the intuitive solution, and it is a complete vindication of the framework's claim that the decision must be taken under the distribution. The deterministic instinct says commit to the best option. The correct answer is often to spread, because the value of the best option depends on the state of the world, and the state of the world is not known. The identical result appears in portfolio theory, where diversification is optimal under uncertainty about future returns for reasons that are formally the same, and in Bayesian experimental design, where randomisation is a way of buying information.

**Bet hedging versus optimisation, and the decision.** Since the framework recommends hedging and the deterministic instinct recommends committing, the resolution is to hedge exactly to the extent of the uncertainty and no further. Hedging more than the uncertainty warrants is a failure to exploit; hedging less is a concentration of risk purchased with false precision. This is the Kelly criterion, and it is the correct general answer, and it is a bounded amount of commitment rather than a choice between extremes.

**The units of adaptation.** A population is a memory that no individual possesses, and it learns faster than any individual can, because it maintains many hypotheses at once. The general lesson for engineered systems is that maintaining a distribution of hypotheses can beat the best single hypothesis under a computational or a sample budget, and that this is often observed in practice and seldom explained.

**Red Queen dynamics.** Under sustained environmental change the fitness of a trait declines without any change in the trait, because the environment moves. A system under this dynamic is permanently behind, permanently improving, and permanently at a fixed distance from where it would need to be to be safe. The framework's prediction is that a system in this regime should never stop exploring and should never be confident, and that the correct operating point is a permanently maintained distribution. Any system that reaches a confident fixed point in a Red Queen environment is already losing.

### 8.12 Ecology

**Determinism and unpredictability are different, and the logistic map is the demonstration.** The logistic map is deterministic and exhibits chaos, so the correct response is not a distribution over a noise process but a statement about a finite predictability horizon. This distinction matters practically. A deterministic chaotic system is not subject to the sampling error that limits a noisy one, and the two require different experimental designs: the chaotic system needs more precise initial conditions, the noisy one needs more observations.

**Extinction risk is a distributional question, and this is the field's central result.** The time to extinction of an isolated population is a random variable with a distribution, and the mean time is not the quantity that matters. A population of a hundred has a distribution of extinction times with a long tail, and an intervention that improves the mean but not the tail has not reduced extinction risk. The extinction vortex, in which smallness increases the variance of demographic rates which increases smallness, is a self reinforcing loss of the buffer that makes variance tolerable. The policy implication is that the small population is the one at risk, and that the appropriate metric is a quantile of the extinction time distribution, not its mean.

**Allee effects and the two way nature of the intervention.** A population below a critical density declines toward extinction with high probability, and a density above it grows. The system has two basins, and the current state does not determine the future because the basin does. This is the clearest example in the biological sciences of a system that cannot be specified by a point, and the policy consequence is that the quantity to preserve is the distance to the basin boundary, which is a geometric quantity about a belief rather than a scalar about a state.

**Functional redundancy as insurance.** An ecosystem's response to a perturbation is determined by which functional roles are represented and whether more than one species fills each. This is a portfolio argument, and the conclusion, that diversity is insurance, follows from the same mathematics as diversification in finance, and the empirical support is the observed collapse of function following the removal of a few components from systems thought to be redundant.

**Adaptive management.** The formal structure of adaptive management in conservation is the framework exactly, with uncertainty about the state, an action, an observation, and an update, and a decision rule that maximises expected long term value under the current belief. Its central practical finding is the one that should be general: the option value of waiting for information is positive, and a policy that commits early is usually wrong even when the early information is better, because it forecloses the response to what the later information would have revealed. The cost of irreversibility is what the option value buys.

### 8.13 Systems biology and causality

**The identifiability problem is severe and it is structural.** The same time course of a gene regulatory network is consistent with many topologies. A static network topology is an idealisation of a system whose real interactions are conditional on state, and the number of possible conditionals is far beyond what any time course can constrain. The honest output is a posterior over networks, and reporting a single inferred network with confidence intervals on the edges is standard practice and is not what the identifiability supports.

**Intervention is the only source of the counterfactual, and this is the practical meaning of causality.** Passive observation reveals correlations induced by the current policy, which is a biased sample of the space of behaviours. Perturbation reveals the response, which is the thing anyone actually wants to know. The framework's exploration term is exactly this, and the criterion for a good experiment is that it reduces the posterior entropy over the mechanism, not that it produces a large effect.

**The identification problem, stated in the general form.** Given inputs, outputs and a restricted model class, there is a family of parameter values that give the same input output map, and these are observationally equivalent. Where a direction in parameter space is unconstrained by the data, the parameter is not identifiable, and it will nonetheless appear in a fitted model with a confidence interval computed under an assumption that was never tested. The correction is to change the experiment, or to report the unidentifiable directions as unidentifiable. Reporting them as estimated is the most common quantitative overreach in the empirical sciences and it is entirely diagnosable.

### 8.14 Epidemiology

**Deterministic SIR is wrong at exactly the moment it matters.** A deterministic model of an outbreak cannot produce an epidemic that dies out, and it cannot produce the stochastic failure of a seeding event, which is the defining question of the early phase of any emerging outbreak. The correct framework for the early phase is a branching process, in which each infected individual independently generates a number of secondary infections, that number is itself a random variable, and the probability that an outbreak dies out is computable from the distribution. This is the most consequential single correction in the field, because the early phase determines whether there is a pandemic at all, and a deterministic model would confidently predict a major outbreak every time one seeds.

**The offspring number is a distribution, and its variance is the operational quantity.** The mean of the offspring number determines whether sustained spread is possible. The variance determines whether it is likely. An outbreak with the same mean as one that reliably causes pandemics but with much greater variance has a substantial probability of fizzling, and that variance is what the epidemiological literature calls overdispersion, and it is produced by heterogeneity in contact rate, in susceptibility, in the timing of the infectious period, and in the social structure of the population. The operational consequence is that a large fraction of introductions die out, and a policy that does not account for that will appear to fail repeatedly, and the failures will be interpreted as compliance problems rather than as the arithmetic of branching processes.

**The effective reproduction number is a random variable, not a number.** It varies with the state of the population, with the behaviour of individuals, and with the season, and it is measured with substantial uncertainty at every point in time. A policy that treats it as a scalar will respond too slowly when it is falling and too aggressively when it is uncertain. Its variance is what determines the probability that an intervention is needed, and a policy designed against the mean is a policy designed against the wrong quantity.

**Flattening is a statement about a distribution and was argued as one.** The objective was never to reduce the mean number of infections, which was already falling for uninteresting reasons. It was to change the shape of the distribution, to move mass from the upper tail into the body, because the objective function was hospitalisation, which is a threshold phenomenon, and threshold phenomena are controlled by the tail and not the mean. This is the sharpest general lesson in the document: **when the harm is produced by a threshold, optimising the mean is optimising the wrong thing.** The same structure appears in the design of any safety limit, in the design of any insurance pool, and in any setting where the consequence is triggered by an extreme rather than by an average.

**The counterfactual problem is not optional.** Every statement about whether an intervention worked is a statement about what would have happened without it, and there is no observation of that counterfactual. The modelling is therefore not a convenience layered on top of the evidence, it is the only route to the question, and the honest statement of a policy's effect is a posterior over counterfactual trajectories. The result is that the confidence interval on a policy's effect is dominated by the uncertainty in the counterfactual rather than by the measurement error, and interventions that reduce the size of the counterfactual set, such as randomised allocation, are disproportionately valuable per unit of effort. The Lucas critique makes the same point from the other side: a policy rule whose reaction function is understood and anticipated becomes ineffective, which is the model specification problem appearing in a policy setting, and it means that any policy which is transparent about its own rule will degrade.

**Interventions change the thing they measure.** Testing, masking, isolation and vaccination all change contact patterns, and therefore change the reproduction number they are being evaluated by. This is not a philosophical point about performativity, it is a sampling statement: the intervention alters the distribution of states that the surveillance observes. The practical implication is that randomised and stepped wedge designs exist not merely for fairness but because they are the only designs that preserve some of the counterfactual information, and the loss of that information is the price of a well intentioned rollout.

### 8.15 Climatology

**The deterministic skeleton is excellent and the distribution is the science.** The energy balance of the planet is a deterministic and well understood set of equations, and the observed record agrees with them. What is not deterministic is the internal variability, the ocean circulation, the ice albedo feedback, and the response of the carbon cycle, and these produce a spread of possible trajectories. The forecast is therefore a distribution and the spread is the forecast, and a deterministic projection presented as a single number is a category error even when the underlying model is good.

**Calibration of the ensemble is the central methodological requirement, and it is testable.** A forecast system is calibrated when the spread of its ensemble matches the error it subsequently makes, so that in a large sample, the observed outcomes fall inside the ensemble in the proportion the ensemble claims. This is directly checkable over many forecasts, and it is the natural home of the calibration requirement from section 4.7. Most forecasting systems are, at various times, mis calibrated, and the direction of the error is usually overconfidence, and the consequence is a forecast whose central track is treated as more reliable than it is.

**Non stationarity is a structural feature and not a nuisance.** The forcing changes, the observing system changes, and the ocean state has its own multi decade variability that can be mistaken for a trend. Any statistical method that assumes stationarity will produce a spurious trend somewhere, and the defence is a careful treatment of the observational system and of the internal variability, and the acknowledgement that a trend and a phase of natural variability are not always separable with the data available.

**The decision is made on the distribution of the consequence, not on the central projection.** The relevant question is not what the expected warming is but what the probability is of a severe outcome, and the loss function that matters includes irreversible and catastrophic terms, which by construction are not captured by an expectation. Tail dependence in a joint distribution of temperature, sea level, ice and precipitation is the object of study, and a marginal distribution is not sufficient to reason about it.

### 8.16 Meteorology

Ensemble prediction is the largest scale deployment of the framework in existence, and the vocabulary is different but the structure is the same. The equations are deterministic, the atmosphere is chaotic, the initial state is uncertain, and the forecast is a probability distribution over a horizon. Beyond the predictability horizon the honest output is the distribution and nothing else.

The operational discipline is calibration, maintained continuously, by verifying that the spread of the ensemble matches the observed error. The lesson of the field is that the value of a probabilistic forecast is realised only at the point of decision, and that a probabilistic forecast communicated to a decision maker who has been trained to want a single number will be converted back into a single number, and the conversion is where the information is destroyed. This is an organisational problem as much as a technical one, and it is the same problem as a physician receiving a posterior and reporting a verdict.

### 8.17 Seismology

**The deterministic goal is abandoned in favour of a hazard curve, and that transition is the paradigm case.** Earthquake prediction, meaning a time and a place and a magnitude, is not achievable. What is achievable and is what building codes actually use is a hazard curve, giving the probability of various levels of shaking at a site in a time window, derived from a recurrence model with parameters estimated from a catalogue. The whole discipline moved from prediction to hazard, and the engineering benefit of that move is enormous while the scientific content of the abandoned goal was nil.

**Catalogues are biased samples and the bias is time varying.** Detection capability depends on the instrumentation, and networks change, and regions change. A catalogue from a modern global network is not a random sample of events, it is heavily biased toward recent, large, well instrumented events, and any rate estimated from it must account for that. This is a non stationary observation process and the estimation has to treat it as such, and the failure to do so has produced real errors in hazard assessments.

**Declustering is a decision under uncertainty that has to be made before anything else can be estimated.** Because aftershock sequences violate Poisson statistics, the rate calculation requires choosing how to treat the sequences, and different choices give different rates. The choice is an assumption about the process, it is consequential, and the honest practice is to propagate it.

**Aftershock decay has an empirically established law with estimated parameters.** The decay of aftershock rate with time is a specific functional form, and the parameters are estimated, and the estimation is difficult because the sequences are short and the largest events are rare. The prediction performance of a fitted law over a held out sequence is a genuine out of sample test, and the field performs it.

### 8.18 Oceanography

**Tides are deterministic and internal waves are not, in the same system and the same ocean.** The tidal prediction is a harmonic calculation that works to high accuracy. The internal wave field is a random process. A single ocean contains both, and the deterministic instinct applied to the whole ocean produces confident nonsense about the internal variability.

**Reconstruction from sparse observations is a posterior over fields.** Argo floats sample the ocean at a resolution that is far coarser than the structures of interest, and the three dimensional field is inferred from a sparse and irregular set of observations through a dynamical model. The result is an estimate with spatially structured uncertainty, and the uncertainty is correlated across space because the dynamics propagate information. Quoting an independent error at each point understates the confidence in large scale structure, which is a systematic and well known error in oceanographic products.

**The overturning circulation is a slow stochastic system with a possible discontinuity.** Whether the Atlantic overturning can collapse within a plausible horizon is an active question, and it is a question about the tail of a distribution under uncertainty, not about a central projection. Model disagreement across the published models is informative about the uncertainty, and treating the spread as noise to be averaged away would discard the actual information content of the disagreement.

### 8.19 Geology and resource estimation

**Dating is an inference with a distribution.** Every age is a posterior over a range, with contributions from the measurement, from the calibration curve, and from the geological model of the event being dated. Quoting an age without its uncertainty is quoting a projection, and for a geochronology sample the uncertainty is often comparable to the quantity of interest.

**Geological interpretation is an inverse problem with non unique solutions.** A seismic survey or a borehole programme constrains a subsurface model, and many models are consistent with the data. The correct product is a distribution over models, and the practice of producing a single interpreted cross section is a presentation convenience that conceals a genuine and sometimes enormous ambiguity.

**Mineral resource estimation is the clearest commercial case for probabilistic reporting.** The value of a deposit is a function of the uncertainty in the ore body, the extraction cost, and the commodity price, and because the value function is nonlinear, the value distribution is strongly right skewed and the median is far below the mean. An industry that reports a single expected net present value is reporting a number that no mine will earn and that no one should finance against. The correct report is a distribution with stated percentiles, and the shift of valuation practice toward reporting ranges is a real and welcome application of exactly the argument in section 8.14, in a different century, about a different loss function.

### 8.20 Planetary science and exoplanets

**Retrieval is the paradigm inference problem and it is frequently degenerate.** Inferring atmospheric composition, temperature, or cloud structure from a measured spectrum is a forward model run backwards, and the inversion is often poorly constrained. In the most striking cases, a single directly imaged exoplanet has a retrieved posterior so broad that no single atmospheric model is consistent with the data, and the correct output is the entire family. The temptation to quote the best fitting model as the atmosphere is the deterministic failure, and in this subfield it has been the source of well publicised overreach.

**The correct presentation is the marginals, the correlations, and the information content.** A retrieval should report which parameters are constrained, which are not, which are correlated with which, and what additional observation would most reduce the remaining uncertainty. That last quantity, the value of a proposed follow up observation, is a measurement design calculation, and it is the natural way to rank observing proposals.

**Planetary protection and the cost of an irreversible act.** Some observations are cheap and some destroy the possibility of others, and the framework's treatment of irreversible commitment, the Kelly argument and the option value of waiting, applies directly. It is a case where the correct decision rule is not the one that maximises the expected value of the measurement.

### 8.21 Materials science and nanotechnology

**Deterministic placement loses to statistical assembly at small scale.** Above some feature size, a lithographic process that specifies each position can control the structure. Below it, thermal fluctuation sets the limit on what any process can achieve, and the outcome of a synthesis is a distribution over structures with a yield, and the design question becomes the design of a distribution. A chip yield is a statement about a binomial or Poisson process, and a manufacturing organisation that reports a single yield number rather than a distribution of outcomes across a wafer is discarding the information that would let it find the mechanism of its losses.

**The error in nanometre lithography is thermal, and so it is a noise problem.** The remedy is not a more deterministic process but a process whose distribution is narrow enough, which is a different engineering objective and is achieved by reducing the random component rather than by increasing the precision of the intended component.

**Feedback controlled self assembly is adaptive control with a real controller.** Directed self assembly, where a field guides assembly, is closed loop because the outcome is measured and fed back, and the measurement step is the same for every technique. The interesting question in this field is the resolution of the feedback, since the measurement and actuation have a scale and a latency, and the reachable precision is bounded by the same dynamics that constrain any control system. This is the framework applied to matter.

### 8.22 Crystallography

**This field is the canonical demonstration that observation is incomplete.** Diffraction gives the intensities of the reflections, which are the squared magnitudes of the Fourier transform of the electron density, and the phases are not measured. The structure is therefore not observed but inferred, and the phase problem is the cleanest large scale example of a measurement function with a kernel. The entire practice of the field is the construction of a prior over plausible structures and its refinement against the measured intensities, and the fact that this works at all is evidence for the framework's central claim that inference from partial observations is the normal case rather than an emergency.

**The experimental loop is designed under a belief.** Each diffraction experiment is chosen to maximise the information it provides about the current best structure hypothesis, and the practice of collecting a redundant or apparently wasteful second crystal to resolve a specific ambiguity is optimal experimental design in its most literal form.

**R factors are a calibration statistic, and their limitations are known.** The residual between observed and calculated intensities depends on the resolution, the multiplicity, the model and the data collection strategy, and the discipline's own awareness that the number is not comparable across structures collected differently is an instance of the calibration requirement in section 4.7.

### 8.23 Polymer science

**The molecular weight distribution is the object, and the averages are summaries of it.** The most probable distribution, the number average and the weight average differ, and they differ in a way that reflects the shape of the distribution, so a sample with a given number average and a given dispersity can have a very different mechanical behaviour from a sample with the same number average and a different dispersity. Reporting one average is reporting a projection of a distribution whose shape carries the function.

**Non equilibrium response depends on rate, which is the absence of a state function.** A polymer being stretched is not described by a state, it is described by a history, and the same material under a different protocol ends in a different configuration. This is a general and important point that extends well beyond polymers: **a system with memory cannot be specified by a point, and any model that assigns it one is approximating.** Relaxation, memory effects and hysteresis are all the same phenomenon, and they are the reason that the phrase "the state of the system" is unsafe in any setting where the history is not negligible.

**Non Arrhenius dynamics make extrapolation dangerous.** Near a glass transition, relaxation is not exponential in inverse temperature, so an Arrhenius fit to data within one regime will predict nonsense outside it. This is the generic failure of extrapolating a model beyond the region where it was validated, and it is worth naming because it recurs in every field: reliability, pharmacology, materials, economics, climate. The correct procedure is to state the validated range and refuse to report outside it.

### 8.24 Semiconductor physics

**The subthreshold slope limit is the field's founding demonstration that a point target is unreachable.** In a conventional transistor, the gate voltage required to change the current by a factor of ten has a floor of about sixty millivolts at room temperature, and the floor comes from thermodynamics and quantum mechanics together, and it is not an engineering deficiency that better fabrication will remove. The industry did not defeat the limit. It changed the objective, from driving a current to zero, which is impossible, to driving the distribution of on and off states to a favourable ratio, which is achievable, and then used that distribution as the representation of the bit. The design variable became a probability rather than a voltage, and the field that followed, probabilistic and analogue computing, is the same conceptual move applied at the level of architecture: the state of a bit is a distribution over physical configurations, the error rate is a first class design parameter, and the algorithm is chosen to be robust to that error rate rather than to assume its absence.

**A probabilistic processor does not compute a value, it computes a distribution over values.** This changes the design of every layer above it. Redundancy is no longer waste, it is the mechanism by which the distribution is concentrated. An algorithm for such a machine is not a program, it is a scheme for arriving at a correct answer given a specified failure rate, which is a different intellectual object, and it is the same shift that appears in error corrected classical computation and in probabilistic programming languages.

**Defect statistics and yield as a probabilistic discipline.** Yield is a measured distribution over a population of devices, and the physical models that predict it are models of defect generation, and the entire economics of the industry is a comparison between the cost of a defect and the value of the yield. The framework's contribution here is to insist that the yield distribution is a first class object and that optimising a mean yield while ignoring the tail produces a product whose worst devices are far worse than the mean suggests.

### 8.25 Neuroscience

The brain is the largest and most instructive deployment of the framework, and the case is made stronger by the fact that the deterministic model was tried first and did not survive contact with data.

**Neurons are noisy, and this is not a small correction.** Spiking is a point process, and the variability in the timing of spikes is comparable in magnitude to the variability in the quantity being encoded. The deterministic integrate and fire neuron was a modelling convenience, and the empirical situation is that variability in single neurons is often functionally significant rather than merely nuisance. The consequences run through the whole field. A conclusion drawn from the mean response is not a conclusion about the system, and the standard statistical apparatus of the field, which compares conditions with an analysis of variance across trials, is itself an acknowledgement of this.

**Population coding is the paradigm probabilistic result.** In many visual and auditory areas, information about a stimulus is carried not by the response of any single neuron, whose variability would make its signal unusable, but by the pattern of responses across a population, and the population response is used as a distribution from which a decoder infers the stimulus. The system's output is genuinely a distribution, and the reading of that distribution is the computation. There is no single neuron whose value is the perception.

**Perception as inference, and the prior as accumulated belief.** The Bayesian brain account treats perception as the combination of prior belief with likelihood, with the prior supplied by accumulated experience. The most striking and uncomfortable consequence is the demonstration that the perceived value of a stimulus is shifted by the prior, and shifted *toward* the prior, which is a statement about how much of perception is inference rather than measurement. The philosophical reading, that we do not see the world, and that what we see is a hypothesis, is a direct consequence of the framework rather than a separate metaphysical claim.

**Reinforcement learning is the framework in its biologically implemented form.** The learning signal is the temporal difference error, the value estimate is revised toward the observed outcome in proportion to the error, and this has been verified in the dopamine system, where the phasic response of dopamine neurons to a reward prediction error matches the prediction of the model quantitatively. The learning rate is not arbitrary, and there is a specific role played by the rate at which a stimulus is predicted: a stimulus that is reliably predicted produces no phasic response and therefore no learning, and this is the neural implementation of the point in section 4.4 that information is what is valuable, not contact. Uncertainty and risk are separately represented in the forebrain and basal ganglia, and the behavioural pharmacology of this system is the experimental confirmation that they are computational variables.

**Foraging.** The evidence accumulation account of decision making is a probabilistic model in which a decision is taken when evidence crosses a bound, and the resulting distribution of reaction times is skewed. The correct inference is a drift diffusion model, whose parameters are latent and must be estimated, and the estimation problem for these parameters is a genuine and well studied inverse problem with a hierarchical structure across subjects. The lesson is the framework's: the observable is a distribution of latencies, and the latent variables of the model are the real parameters, and quoting the mean latency as the measure of the decision would discard everything.

**Exploration, exploitation and the value of information in a real system.** Foraging animals under uncertainty do not commit to the best known patch. The experiments that established this are among the cleanest measurements of an information value in a vertebrate, using the controlled manipulation of patch reliability with the option to sample, and the animals' behaviour tracks the value of information quantitatively. Nothing about this is metaphorical, and the behaviour is a near optimal solution of the sequential problem in section 4.4.

**Plasticity, stability and sleep.** The stability and plasticity problem is the framework's Law 3 in the brain: the same plasticity rule must support learning and must not destroy what is already learned. The resolution in the standard accounts involves distinct fast and slow learning systems and an offline consolidation process, in which recent experience is replayed. The correspondence to the separation of the fast and slow loops is close, and the function of the offline phase is the same function, namely to integrate new evidence into a stable structure without destroying that structure in the process.

**Predictive processing, and the honest note.** The predictive processing framework and the free energy formulation have become influential in theoretical neuroscience and they are the framework's structure in a cognitive setting. They are also contested, and the specific contested claim, that this framing is a theory of the brain rather than a useful statistical model of perception, has not been settled. The relevant caution for this document is the general one: a formalism that works as a model of behaviour is not thereby a model of the mechanism, and the evidence that distinguishes them is intervention on the mechanism rather than fit to the data.

### 8.26 A note on the method that unifies all of this

Every discipline above arrived at the same structure through its own route, and the routes have a common feature worth naming, because it is the part that transfers.

In every case the sequence was the same. There was first a deterministic account that worked and then failed. There was then a long period in which the failure was patched, restricted to a special case, or explained away, and this period is where most of the wasted effort in every field has been spent. There was finally an abandonment of the deterministic framing for a distributional one, usually forced by a specific phenomenon that would not be accommodated: an extinction in a branching process, a chaos horizon in a weather forecast, an impossible minimisation in a transistor, a structure factor with no phase information, a repeated expression profile, a fat tailed loss, a noise floor in an interferometer. In each case the specific phenomenon was not an inconvenience to be modelled. It was the finding that revealed the framing was wrong.

The transferable lesson is therefore a methodological one, and it is the most useful thing in this document. **When a deterministic model fails in a specific, reproducible way, the correct response is to examine the framing rather than to enlarge the model.** The history of the transition in section 3 is a catalogue of cases where the enlargement approach was tried and where, eventually, it was abandoned, and the abandonment is always the point at which progress became fast.

There is a second methodological lesson, and it is the one that keeps the first from becoming folklore. The specific phenomenon that forced the reframing was, in every case, found by somebody following a procedure carefully enough for the anomaly to survive. The failure was detected by an instrument that was working, reported by someone whose method was written down, and attributed to the framing rather than absorbed into a larger model. None of those four things is guaranteed by effort alone, and all four are habits that can be lost. A discipline that forgets how its last anomaly was found will find its next one late, and will in the meantime have a large, confident, wrong model. This is why the sequence in the paragraph above is worth more than any of the field results that instantiate it, and why the register in section 11 is part of the specification rather than an appendix to it.

## 9. Limits, objections, and what would falsify this

A document claiming a general law should carry the strongest available objections and a test that could fail. Here they are.

### 9.1 The components are not new

This is the most important objection and it is conceded at the start. Bayesian updating, sufficient statistics, sequential analysis, optimal experimental design, estimation and control separation, partially observable control, bandit theory, adaptive control, robust statistics, decision theory and the standard error of a mean are all existing, rigorous, well tested results. This document does not improve any of them.

What is offered is the claim that they compose, that the composition is forced rather than convenient, and that the composed object is transferable to fields it was not derived in, with the field translations given in section 8. The claim of transferability is the substantive part, and it is the part that section 9.6 is designed to test.

### 9.2 No free lunch

There is no free lunch in this area, and the framework does not evade the theorem. Every result about adaptive behaviour is a result about a trade against something. Uniform sampling needs more samples. Exploration costs present value. Adaptation costs stability. Measurement costs money, energy and disturbance. Robustness costs peak performance. Every one of these costs is real, and a design that claims a free version of any of them is either mistaken or has moved the cost somewhere invisible. The honest form of the framework is a cost function and an optimum, and the optimum is a balance point, not a maximum.

### 9.3 When is a deterministic model genuinely correct?

Frequently, and the framework must not be read as a claim that it never is. A rigid body under a known force, a batch chemical reactor with tight mixing, a well characterised electrical circuit in its operating regime, an arithmetic operation, a scheduling problem with known durations, a thermostat with a well characterised plant. In these cases the deterministic model is correct, the state is nearly observable, the disturbance is small, and the deterministic solution is the right answer. What is wrong in these cases is not the model but the claim that the model works outside the regime in which its assumptions hold, and in particular outside the range of validity of the linearisation, the mixing assumption, the calibration, or the horizon.

The framework applies to a deterministic system exactly when one of the four conditions of section 2.2 fails, and the honest diagnostic is to ask which condition and to document the answer.

### 9.4 The cost of adaptation can exceed its benefit

Systems that adapt too eagerly are a real and well documented pathology, and the pathologies are recognisable. Overfitting to noise. Thrashing between regimes. Abandoning a good model because a transient looked like a change. Learning a disturbance as a parameter. In control, a fast identification loop driven by noise is a classic and extensively documented oscillation failure. In statistics, an adaptive procedure that searches hard enough will find a structure that is not there, and this is a statement about the multiple comparisons problem, which is the statistical form of the same pathology.

The cure is the separation of the two rates, the requirement of persistent evidence before a parameter revision, and the calibration test. And the recognition that the exploration budget must be finite, proportional to the edge, and deliberately maintained.

### 9.5 The framework says nothing about the ethical or the political

It is a description of how a system that cannot see what it acts on must be built. It does not tell you what to build, and it does not make the choices about who bears the cost of being wrong, what loss function is legitimate, whose uncertainty counts, or what is worth optimising at all. Those choices are made outside the framework and they are the ones that matter most.

There is one methodological caution that belongs here. A framework that says "optimise the expected value under your belief" can be used to launder a choice of objective. The demand that the loss function be written down, and defended, and examined for whether achieving it fully would leave the world better, is in this document precisely because that demand is the one that is most often skipped.

### 9.6 What would falsify the claim

The central claim is that the same four laws apply in every field, and that the field specific formulations in section 8 are consequences rather than analogies. That claim is falsifiable in the following ways, and each would be a genuine refutation.

**A domain is found where the four conditions of section 2.2 all hold and the deterministic treatment is not merely adequate but optimal, and where the distributional treatment gives no improvement.** This would be a small counterexample rather than a fatal one, since the claim is about the general case, but a substantial and well studied such domain would force a redefinition of the claim's scope.

**A field is found where the best practice is a single point estimate with no uncertainty, and where that is demonstrably superior.** This would indicate that the residual of Law 2 can be made negligible in a way not accounted for here.

**A case is found where maximal exploration is optimal, and where the exploration budget should not be bounded.** This would refute the Kelly argument and the claim that overcommitment is asymmetric. It would require an environment in which the horizon is unbounded and the option to resume does not decay.

**The composed specification is found to be substantially less useful than the separate existing results in some field, and this is demonstrated rather than asserted.** This is the most likely form of failure, and it would be a failure of transferability rather than of the mathematics. The fair test is whether an engineer new to a field, given only sections 1 to 6, makes better design choices than they would have made with the domain literature alone. That is a measurable comparison and it is the one I would propose.

**A large body of well established practice is found to consist of systems that violate the four laws and are nevertheless reliable.** The candidate categories are the ones where humans are systematically poor, since a system that beats human performance by being better at the framework's rules may indicate that the rules describe a specific failure mode of human reasoning rather than a general structure. This objection is serious and it deserves a serious answer, which is that the framework's rules are the ones that survive contact with a hostile environment over a long horizon, and that human performance in these tasks is the best available evidence that this matters, since humans are the only systems we can observe failing at it.

**A documented case is found where a routine is being followed exactly, its preconditions have demonstrably failed, and the system produced a wrong result that its own audit did not catch.** This is the falsification criterion for Law 4 and it is deliberately strict. The objection to Law 4 is that a register is a form of paperwork that cannot be perfect, so it merely relocates the failure from one kind of system to a slightly more self-aware one. That objection is correct, and the correct reply is that Law 4 does not claim to prevent error, only to make error attributable. The register does not stop a method from failing outside its domain. It makes the failure a detectable event rather than an undetectable one, and the difference between those two is the entire practical content of the claim. If a case is found where a fully audited routine failed invisibly, this law is wrong.

## 10. Implementation, in the order that matters

For anyone building against this, the following order is the one that produces reliable results.

**Separate the estimator from the actor explicitly.** If you cannot state what the system believes, independent of what it is about to do, you do not have a system you can reason about. Most subtle errors in this area are a belief and an objective entangled in one function, where a change to the objective silently changes the measurement.

**Publish the measurement function with every number.** What is the instrument, what is its calibration, what does it respond to, what does it not respond to. A number without a measurement function is an assertion.

**Report distributions, and report the correlations.** Especially the correlations. Independent error bars on a family of related quantities are almost always wrong, and they are wrong in a way that inflates apparent certainty in the directions that matter most.

**Report the calibration, not just the accuracy.** And when calibration is poor, fix it before fixing accuracy, because calibration is what the decision maker actually acts on.

**Make the uncertainty reach the decision.** Apply the test in section 4.6. If the same measurement at two different uncertainties produces the same action, the uncertainty is not reaching anything.

**Separate the fast and slow loops, and set the slow one deliberately.** Estimate the timescale over which the environment's parameters actually change, and set the slow loop well below it, accepting the lag as the price of not learning disturbances.

**Match the loss function to the mechanism of harm.** Ask whether the harm is produced by a mean, a tail, a threshold, or a rate, and build the loss accordingly. Using a mean loss against a tail driven mechanism is the most consequential single error in the list.

**Allocate a finite, explicit exploration budget.** And keep it. A system that stops exploring on the grounds that it is now confident has converted its exploration into nothing, and the conversion is not reversible.

**Define the fixed point before you need it.** Decide in advance what the system's steady state looks like and what the revisions look like when it is there, so that the diagnosis of section 5 is available when the system misbehaves rather than being reconstructed afterwards.

**Test out of sample, on the specific prediction.** Not on fit. The fields that got this right, including cosmology and the prediction of new particle properties, did so by making a prediction in advance and being willing to be wrong about it.

**Watch for Goodhart as a matter of routine.** Once the objective is being optimised, ask what it is now a proxy for. The answer will change over time, and the system will not notice, because the score will keep improving.

**Write the method down before you run it, and keep the register.** The pre-registration, the measurement description, the calibration record, the precondition check, the failure log. Not as documentation for its own sake, but because a method that was decided while looking at the results is not a method, it is a rationalisation, and the difference is invisible from inside. This is the whole of Law 4 and it is the only entry in this list that has to be satisfied before anything else in it is worth doing.

**Check the imported results against section 11.** Every technique in this document that came from another field carries an entry recording where it stops being valid. Read the entry before you adopt the technique, not after it has produced an answer. The most expensive mistake available to you is not using a technique outside its domain; it is using one there, getting a result you trust, and having no check that could have told you.

## 11. The register: every borrowed result, its domain, and the check

A document that composes results from game theory, statistics, control theory, physics, chemistry, biology and the social sciences is only trustworthy if it carries its own liabilities. Every technique below is used somewhere in this document. Each entry gives what it assumes, where it holds, and the check that detects when it has stopped holding. An entry without a check is a technique being used on faith, which is the thing this section exists to prevent.

The checks are deliberately chosen to be cheap and mechanical. A check that requires as much work as the method is not a check. In every case, the check is something you can compute, observe or falsify, and in every case, the cost of running it is small relative to the cost of not running it.

**Bayes' rule and conditional inversion.** *Assumes* a correct likelihood and a stated prior, and that the prior is not doing work you have not admitted. *Holds* wherever the likelihood is specified, always, which is precisely the problem: it is available everywhere. *Check* the sensitivity of the conclusion to the prior. A conclusion that reverses when the prior is moved within a defensible range is a conclusion about the prior, and must be reported as one.

**Sufficient statistic.** *Assumes* the statistic retains everything relevant, which is a claim about the measurement, not about the algebra. *Holds* for a fixed model class; sufficiency is relative to the model you assume, so a statistic sufficient under a Gaussian model is not sufficient under a heavy tailed one. *Check* refit under an alternative model class and confirm the statistic is unchanged. If it changes, the sufficiency was a property of the assumption.

**Cramér Rao bound.** *Assumes* unbiasedness, and local rather than global regularity. *Holds* for unbiased estimators of a regular parameter. *Check* whether the estimator is biased before invoking the bound as a floor. A biased estimator is not bounded by it, and routinely beats it, which is a legitimate method for some problems and a hidden defect in others.

**Fisher information and optimal experimental design.** *Assumes* a parametric model and a cost function for the measurement. *Holds* when both are right, and the design is only optimal for the model supplied. *Check* design the experiment under a deliberately wrong model and see whether the result survives. If the design is robust to that, the model assumption was not doing the work.

**Wald's sequential analysis.** *Assumes* a test that stops early still controls the error rate over the whole stopping region, not at one fixed sample size. *Holds* when that uniformity is achieved, which requires the stopping rule to be derived rather than chosen. *Check* run the procedure under simulated data with the true effect exactly at the boundary, which is where an optional stopping error shows up first.

**The Gittins index.** *Assumes* a discounted or finite horizon problem with Markov arms, and that the index is computable. *Holds* for that problem class. Computing it exactly is intractable in general, so practical implementations approximate it, and the approximation is where the error enters. *Check* the index against brute force on a small instance. If it is implemented as a heuristic and not verified, the theory is decorative.

**Thompson sampling.** *Assumes* the posterior is the one you should be sampling from, and that the reward structure is not adversarially chosen after the prior is fixed. *Holds* broadly, which is why it survived from 1933. *Check* the performance is not driven by a small number of lucky samples. Run multiple seeds and report the spread, because a single seed reports the best draw, not the method.

**UCT and Monte Carlo tree search.** *Assumes* a generative model or simulator, a finite or discounted horizon, and rewards in a bounded range. *Holds* under those conditions, with an important caveat that is frequently forgotten: the asymptotic optimality claim in the 2006 Kocsis and Szepesvari paper is admitted to be incomplete by Szepesvari himself, and Coquelin and Munos showed UCT can be too optimistic in some trees, with regret as bad as O(exp(exp(D))) in depth. The rule is heavily used in practice and the practical results are good, but "provably near optimal" is a weaker statement than it is usually quoted as. *Check* do not use it as a proof of optimality. Use it as a search heuristic that happens to work well, and verify the value estimate independently.

**Elo and rating systems.** *Assumes* a single scalar per player, which implies transitivity. *Holds* in games with a strong transitive component, chess and go largely do. *Fails* in intransitive games, rock paper scissors being canonical, and the failure is not a small bias: once transitivity is relaxed, estimated ratings become dependent on who played whom. It also provably fails to rank players correctly even for some genuinely transitive games, and the assumption is routinely violated in the games industry itself, since StarCraft II and Dota are cited as non transitive. *Check* measure the intransitivity of your own payoff matrix before quoting a rating as a skill. A rating is a statement about a pool under a schedule, not a property of a player.

**Kalman filtering.** *Assumes* a linear system, a known transition and measurement model, Gaussian noise, and correct model order. *Holds* when those hold, and the gain is computed from them, so a wrong model produces a confidently wrong gain rather than a noisy one. *Check* observe the innovation sequence, the difference between predicted and observed. It should be white with no structure. Structure in the innovations means the model is wrong, and the filter is absorbing the error as though it were noise.

**The fluctuation dissipation relation.** *Assumes* equilibrium, and applies to a system in equilibrium. *Holds* near equilibrium, and is where it is most famous. *Check* the system is actually in equilibrium before quoting a noise floor from it. Far from equilibrium the relation does not hold, and a noise floor computed from it will be wrong in the direction that makes the measurement look better than it is.

**Jarzynski's equality and the Crooks relation.** *Assumes* that work can be measured exactly along a trajectory, and, in the original formulation, that the system begins in equilibrium. *Holds* as an exact identity for the exponential average, and the cost is severe: because it averages an exponential, the number of trajectories needed grows exponentially with the variance of the work, which is why the method is exact and expensive. *Check* the convergence of the exponential average, not the mean. The mean work converges fast and tells you nothing about whether the free energy estimate is ready.

**The central limit theorem.** *Assumes* independence, or weak dependence with conditions satisfied, and finite variance. *Holds* in the bulk and fails in the tail, which is the entire point of using it and the entire trap. It also assumes enough observations that the limit has kicked in. *Check* plot the residuals rather than the summary statistic, and count the excursions. The tail is where the damage is, and the tail is what the theorem tells you least about.

**Red Queen dynamics and evolutionarily stable strategies.** *Assumes* a fitness function that is a property of the strategy in a fixed environment, and a population large enough for frequencies to be meaningful. *Holds* in the specific models; does not transfer to cases where the fitness itself is being measured. *Check* whether the fitness function is independently measured or inferred from the same data used to show adaptation. Inferred fitness is a restatement of the observation, and an ESS computed from it is circular.

**Gene regulatory network inference from expression data.** *Assumes* the time course identifies the network. *Fails* in exactly that assumption: many topologies produce indistinguishable trajectories, so the honest output is a posterior over networks and a single inferred network with edge confidence intervals is overreach. *Check* perturb. Identifiability improves with intervention, and any conclusion that survives only observational data should be labelled as such.

**Drift diffusion models of decision making.** *Assumes* fixed latent parameters and a specific accumulation process. *Holds* well in laboratory tasks with trained subjects. *Fails* when the parameters drift within the trial or when the evidence is not a scalar, and the fit will still look good because the model is flexible. *Check* hierarchical estimation across subjects, and inspect the between subject spread. A single subject's fit with free parameters is not evidence for the model.

**Dopamine temporal difference signals.** *Assumes* the recorded response is the model prediction rather than something correlated with it. *Holds* for reward prediction error in well established tasks. *Check* the specific prediction, phasic response to a predicted reward should be absent, and its presence or absence is a falsifiable prediction rather than a correlation.

**Kelly criterion sizing.** *Assumes* the edge is real, and that the returns are appropriately modelled, and it assumes the objective is long run log growth. *Holds* under those assumptions. *Fails* when the objective is something other than log growth, and a solvency constraint or a ruin condition invalidates the pure criterion outright. *Check* state the objective before applying the formula. A Kelly fraction computed for a different objective is a different number and a different strategy, and the criterion is popularly quoted without the objective attached.

**Goodhart's law and Campbell's law.** *Assumes* the measure is a proxy and remains so. *Holds* as soon as the measure is optimised. *Check* ask directly what the measure is now a proxy for, on a schedule, in writing. The check is trivial and almost nobody does it, which is the whole point of listing it.

**Reproducibility and pre-registration as a method.** *Assumes* nothing about the world, and is the one entry here that is a control on the method rather than a technique. *Holds* always. *Check* the analysis plan was fixed before the data were seen, and that a competent analyst with the pre-registered plan would reach the reported conclusion from the same data. If not, the gap between the plan and the analysis is where the result came from.

**The pessimistic caveat on all of the above.** None of these checks is complete, none of them is cheap at scale, and a system can pass every one and still be wrong, because the failure modes that matter most are the ones nobody thought to check. This entry exists to prevent the register from being read as a guarantee. The register makes error attributable. It does not make error rare, and any document that claims the second is selling something.

## Appendix: the shortest possible statement

A system cannot see what it acts on. It acts on a belief, and the belief is built from measurements that are themselves imperfect, costly, and biased by whatever the system was doing when it looked. Therefore, hold a distribution rather than a point, choose actions by minimising expected loss under that distribution, measure to reduce uncertainty rather than merely to confirm, keep the fast loop fast and the slow loop slow, match the adaptation rate to the rate of change of the world, and check that your stated confidence matches your observed accuracy. The residual uncertainty never goes away, and a system that behaves as though it has is the one that fails, in the place and at the moment you designed it never to. And because none of that can be checked by looking harder at your own reasoning, write the method down before you run it, record what it assumes and where it stops being true, and when it stops being true say so. The difference between a result and a fabrication is not confidence. It is a record someone else can check.
